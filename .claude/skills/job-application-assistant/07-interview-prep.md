## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

### 1. Automating fund expense reporting (Process automation / initiative)
**S:** At State Street, monthly TNA reporting and management fee calculations for client funds involved a lot of repetitive manual work in Excel.
**T:** Nobody asked him to fix this - he noticed the repetition himself and wanted to free up time for higher-value work.
**A:** Built Excel VBA macros and small Python scripts to automate the recurring parts of the reporting and calculation process.
**R:** Reduced the manual effort needed each reporting cycle and freed up time to focus on client communication and exception handling instead of repetitive data entry.
**Use for:** "Tell me about a time you improved a process", "Tell me about a time you took initiative", "Describe your automation/technical skills"
**Note:** No exact time-saved percentage is recorded - if asked for a number in an interview, describe the change qualitatively (less manual rework, fewer copy-paste errors) rather than inventing a figure.

### 2. Learning new internal systems quickly (Adaptability / fast learning)
**S:** Joining State Street in August 2024 as a Fund Expense Administration Associate required getting productive quickly on several internal systems (MyStateStreet, eHorizon) he had never used before.
**T:** Needed to become reliable on client-facing reporting (TNA, management fees) within a limited ramp-up period.
**A:** Focused on learning the systems hands-on, asking targeted questions, and building his own Excel-based checks to catch mistakes early while still new to the process.
**R:** Became a functioning, client-facing member of the team and later moved from "just learning the process" to actively improving it (see STAR #1).
**Use for:** "How do you approach learning something new?", "Tell me about ramping up in a new role"

### 3. Working under a fast-paced, hands-on retail environment (Freego Market)

**R:** Reliable execution of a operational, detail-heavy role - an early example of comfort with structured, process-driven work.
**Use for:** "Tell me about working in a fast-paced environment", "Describe a detail-oriented task you've handled"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why are you looking to leave State Street?" (currently employed, not "did you leave")
> I've enjoyed the fund administration work and especially the process automation side of it, but I'm looking for a role that lets me grow further - either deeper into financial operations at a similar organization, or into more technical/data-driven work, which is why I'm actively studying SQL, data analysis, and automation tooling right now.

### "You don't have a completed degree / You don't have [SQL / formal data experience]."
> That's fair. I studied Electrical Engineering and Automation for three years without finishing, and I'm currently closing the SQL and data-analysis gap directly through focused, hands-on coursework rather than a classroom. What I do bring is a track record of learning fast on the job - I picked up State Street's internal systems from scratch and went on to automate parts of the reporting process with Excel VBA and Python within my first year.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Ideally in a role where I've grown from individual contributor into someone who can lead or manage a small team, in either fund/financial operations or a more technical/data-driven function - built on the automation and process-improvement work I've already started.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> I don't yet have formal people-management experience, even though I'm drawn to it - so far that's shown up as informally helping colleagues and pushing process improvements rather than leading a team. I'm addressing it by looking for roles with a path toward that responsibility rather than expecting to start there.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize per company. Must reference: specific projects, company values, market position, or team structure. Never give a generic answer.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the development/project lifecycle look like, from idea to production?"
- "How do you onboard new team members?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your current tech stack for [relevant area]?"
- "Is there room to grow into more architectural or strategic decisions?"
- "How does the team stay current with new tools and methods?"

### About Culture (use these to prevent disappointment)
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "What does professional development look like here?"
- "Is there flexibility for remote/hybrid work?"
- "What's the balance between development/new projects and maintenance work?"
- "How would you describe the leadership style in this team?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
